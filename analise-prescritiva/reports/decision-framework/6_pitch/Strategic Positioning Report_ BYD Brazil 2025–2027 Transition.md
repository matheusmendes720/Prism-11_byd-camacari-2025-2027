#### 3\. Financial Vulnerability: The Multiplicative "Double Cost-Shock"

The convergence of regulatory tariff hikes (S10) and foreign exchange volatility (S1) creates a non-linear financial risk. These shocks hit the exact same  **CIF (Cost, Insurance, and Freight)**  base, leading to multiplicative rather than additive cost surges.

##### The 35% Permanent Tariff

In July 2025, Camex formally rejected BYD’s request for tariff reductions on SKD/CKD kits. Consequently, a  **permanent 35% tariff**  on all imported kits will take effect in January 2027\. This is now the baseline financial reality for the Camaçari ramp-up.

##### Double Shock Analysis: The 21% Surge

A simple additive view of a 10% BRL devaluation and a 10-percentage-point tariff hike would suggest a 20% impact. The mathematical reality is multiplicative:  **1.10 (FX) x 1.10 (Tariff) \= 1.21** , a  **21% surge**  in landed cost.

#### 4\. Game Theory Modeling: Nash Equilibrium and Defensive Moats

In high-stakes, low-differentiation environments, we model competitor behavior using a 5-player Game Theory framework (BYD, Stellantis, VW, GM, Geely).

##### Nash Equilibrium (Scenario E3)

The most stable competitive state involves BYD maintaining a  **"HIGH" strategy**  (high pure-EV volume and capital investment) while legacy players (Stellantis, GM, VW) adopt  **"LOW" strategies** . "LOW" in this context refers to a focus on hybrid-flex technology to protect legacy ICE margins while avoiding the capital-heavy pure-EV volume race. This equilibrium allows BYD to capture share, but at the cost of sustaining a permanent defensive pricing posture.

##### Verticalization as an Asymmetric Advantage

BYD must leverage its battery verticalization (LFP cells) to maintain a 15-20% cost advantage over legacy players who must buy cells from third parties (CATL/LG). We must avoid being the catalyst for the price war, instead using our cost moat to survive the deflationary pressure initiated by others.

##### Coalition D (BYD \+ VW)

Modeling suggests a Pareto-superior outcome in a coalition with Volkswagen. By sharing platforms and reducing cannibalization, both players could see a market share improvement of  **5 percentage points**  through optimized segment targeting and shared logistics.
